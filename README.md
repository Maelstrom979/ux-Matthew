# Matthew's UX Journal

**What is Deadlock?**
  Deadlock is a third person hero shooter MOBA (Multiplayer Online Battle Arena) game. There are 6 people on each team with the primary goal being to get to the enemies base and destroy it before they do the same to your own. It is currently in a closed beta state, which means only people who are invited to play the game by one of their friends are able to play it. Given the state the game is in, several criticisms made throughout this are subject to change as the game gets further along in development or fully releases

  When you first open the game, you spawn into a room with the only thing in clear view being a table to interact with to play the game. The issue with this however, is that it doesn’t encourage the player to do any sort of tutorial. In fact, to do the tutorial, you have to walk around the room and interact with a relatively random chair that has the text “Learn To Play” over it.

**Looking at the 3 key elements of Usability, would this be considered a good user experience when first picking up the game?**

  Efficiency: A new user’s experience is efficient in the sense that they do not have to go through a tutorial if they choose not to and likely will not even notice it’s there if they don’t explore a little bit. The tutorial itself is also very short; get to your lane, learn the basic controls, shoot minions, do a small skirmish against enemies that don’t fight back. In theory, this makes the efficiency of a new player’s experience very good, no tutorial is necessary and the tutorial is quick and easy to get through. 

  Effectiveness: The effectiveness of a new player’s experience is however not very good. The shortness of the tutorial and not even guiding the player towards it makes it very inefficient in teaching a new player how the game works. As stated earlier, the genre of the game makes it very difficult for a new player to pick up and fully understand what they are doing, this makes a tutorial almost necessary for a new player. Other games deal with this in a good way by asking a player their experience with either that specific game or games of a similar genre when they first launch the game. Not being a person that makes several accounts for games like this, I’m not certain what the high experience options do but I would assume they skip the tutorial that selecting little-to-no experience forces you into.

  Satisfaction: When I first played the game when it first released in a closed beta 2 years ago, I did not enjoy myself at all, I was not satisfied with anything that happened; the gameplay felt too slow, it felt like nothing happened for the first 15 minutes of the game and I did no damage to my opponents. With it being a combination of two genres that I had played several hundred hours of in both, I was expecting to fall in love with the game but just didn’t. Despite the game being a MOBA that is a relatively slower paced game genre than most competitive games, I expected the damage I dealt to enemies to feel more like the shooter genre that the game also fits into. These aren’t necessarily flaws of the game, it just didn’t fit my mental model of how the game should play out

**What Could Be Improved?**

  My feelings for the game have completely changed after playing the game with other people and spending more time, arguably too long for how short of a time it’s been since I came back, playing the game. There are, however, several things that could be improved for introducing a new player to the game. It’s not necessarily a bad thing that the bots you fight against in the tutorial don’t fight back very much, but it would be very helpful to introduce the player to more of the game’s mechanics during the tutorial: the soul urn, vents, midboss, neutral camps, the different tower variants, winning the game, etc. These could all be done in one tutorial but that would likely be overwhelming for the player and take a relatively long amount of time for one tutorial. I would recommend that the game splits this up into several tutorials that are more digestible, similar to the current tutorial but more in depth with different game mechanics. The game should also encourage the player to do the tutorial more than it currently does, this could be done in several ways: forcing the player to do a certain subset of the tutorials before they can play the game (Not efficient, somewhat satisfying, likely very effective), spawning the player in a spot that is closer to the tutorial interaction than the play table interaction (Efficient, no change in satisfaction, slightly more effective), asking the player’s experience with the game or genres and putting the player in certain tutorials based on their answer (Very efficient, likely more satisfying, effective with the caveat of lying to skip an expected tutorial by some players), or any other number of options.
